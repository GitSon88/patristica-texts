Talmudic References to Jesus (selected public domain passages)
Translation: Public domain renderings of Babylonian Talmud passages traditionally associated with Jesus
Source: Babylonian Talmud (Sanhedrin, etc.), traditional attributions

Sanhedrin 43a (traditional association):
On the eve of the Passover Yeshu was hanged. For forty days before the execution took place, a herald went forth and cried, "He is going forth to be stoned because he has practised sorcery and enticed Israel to apostasy. Any one who can say anything in his favour, let him come forward and plead on his behalf." But since nothing was brought forward in his favour he was hanged on the eve of the Passover.

Sanhedrin 107b / Sotah 47a (traditional association with a figure named Yeshu):
When King Jannaeus put the Rabbis to death, R. Joshua b. Perahiah and Yeshu fled to Alexandria of Egypt. On the resumption of peace, Simeon b. Shetach sent to them: "From me, Jerusalem the holy city, to you, Alexandria of Egypt, my sister. My husband dwells in your midst and I am desolate." Thereupon they arose and came. When they arrived he [R. Joshua] rose and erected a flag for the inn. Yeshu then said: "Master, her eyes are narrow." R. Joshua replied: "Wicked one! Do you occupy yourself with such?" He sent forth four hundred trumpets and excommunicated him.

Note: These passages are the subject of extensive scholarly debate regarding identification, dating, and whether they refer to the Jesus of the New Testament. They are included here as the traditional Talmudic references discussed in historical study of early Christianity.
