# Why this repository does not host *On the Jews and Their Lies*

Luther wrote *Von den Juden und ihren Lügen* in 1543. It is the ugliest thing he
wrote, it was reprinted and used by the Nazis, and both the Lutheran World
Federation (1984) and the ELCA (1994) have formally repudiated it. Patristica's
Luther entry discusses it, and should.

This repository still does not carry the text. That is a deliberate decision,
recorded here so it does not have to be re-argued each time someone finds a copy.

## The problem

There is no public-domain English translation of this tract that is not a
propaganda edition.

- The **1543 German original** is public domain. An English translation is a
  separate copyrightable work.
- The **scholarly English translation** is Martin H. Bertram's, in *Luther's
  Works*, American Edition, vol. 47 (Fortress, 1971). That edition is in
  copyright and is on this project's forbidden list.
- The **English editions that are old enough to be out of copyright** are, so
  far as has been found, antisemitic pamphlets rather than scholarly work.

## The 1948 edition specifically — rejected

A copy of "THE JEWS AND THEIR LIES, Dr Martin Luther, Pub. 1948" was assessed
and rejected. Its copyright page identifies the publisher as the **Christian
Nationalist Crusade**, Los Angeles — Gerald L. K. Smith's organisation, one of
the most prominent antisemitic agitations in mid-century America.

Reasons for rejection, each sufficient on its own:

1. **No named translator.** The introduction describes an anonymous and
   contested translation process and never names the translator. This project's
   rule is that a file whose edition date and translator cannot be established
   does not get added.
2. **Unverifiable fidelity.** A translation commissioned by a hate group to
   advance its agenda cannot be assumed faithful to the 1543 German. An
   inflated rendering would be worse than no text: it would expose the entry to
   the charge of overstating Luther.
3. **Unresolved copyright.** Published 1948, after the 1929 threshold, so its
   status depended on renewal. That could not be established.
4. **It is the weaponisation.** The volume's own framing — the "Jewish problem",
   a companion volume of papal edicts, a claimed plot to suppress the book —
   is antisemitic conspiracy material wrapped around the tract. Hosting it would
   import that framing into a Christian reference guide.

## What to do instead

Quote the tract briefly and directly in the Luther entry, cited to the scholarly
edition. Short quotation for criticism and commentary is a different act from
hosting a distributable file, and it serves the goal better: the reader meets
Luther's actual words inside a frame that names what those words became, rather
than as a clean file that can be lifted out of context.

The entry should state plainly what the document is, when he wrote it, that it
was reprinted and used by the Nazis, and that the Lutheran World Federation
(1984) and the ELCA (1994) have repudiated it.

A reader must be able to see that Luther wrote this, so the entry cannot be
accused of softening him. This app should not also be a clean distribution
point for the tract. Inform, do not weaponise.

## If a suitable source is ever found

It would need a named translator, an establishable publication date, verifiable
public-domain status, and a publisher whose purpose in printing it was not
advocacy. If such an edition surfaces, this decision can be revisited. Until
then, absence is the correct state, and it is not an oversight.
